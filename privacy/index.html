Privacy

Who is responsible

Openshelf is a private, non-commercial project run by Xia Chen, who is responsible for the processing described here (the controller under the GDPR).

Contact: open an issue on GitHub. If your request involves personal details you do not want to make public, open an issue that only says you have a private request, and we will agree on a private channel there.

Visiting the site

The site is hosted on GitHub Pages. When you open a page, GitHub (GitHub B.V., Netherlands, and GitHub, Inc., USA) processes your IP address and the usual request data, such as the page requested, the time and your browser, in order to deliver the page. GitHub states that it logs visitors' IP addresses for security purposes. Openshelf has no access to these logs.

Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR, our legitimate interest in delivering the site securely. GitHub may transfer data to the USA. It is certified under the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework and also uses the EU standard contractual clauses. Details: GitHub's privacy statement.

Cookies and storage in your browser

The site sets no cookies. Fonts, scripts and data all come from this site. The only other server it talks to is our own statistics and voting server, described below. There are no ads or social media embeds.

If you switch the language on the front page, the choice is saved in your browser's local storage under the key openshelf-lang. It stays on your device. Storing it is strictly necessary for the function you asked for (§ 25(2) no. 2 TDDDG). You can delete it in your browser settings at any time.

Links to posts take you to other websites, where their own privacy policies apply.

Usage statistics

We count page views and clicks to learn which posts are read and to improve the site. A small script on the front page sends the following to our server: the page and its language, the website you came from (only its host name, such as news.ycombinator.com), and what you do on the page, for example which post you open and its position in the list, which view, filter or topic you choose, and what you search for. Events from one page load share a random number, which is not stored in your browser.

Your IP address and browser identification (user agent) reach the server with every request. They are not stored. They are only used, together with a secret value and the current date, to compute a short code that lets us count how many different visitors there were on that day. The same visitor cannot be recognised on another day. Cloudflare derives the country from the IP address, and we store the country. Bots are not counted.

If your browser sends Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control, the script sends nothing. Raw records are deleted after 90 days.

The server is a Cloudflare Worker. Cloudflare, Inc. (USA) processes the data on our behalf under its data processing addendum, and the database is located in Western Europe. Cloudflare is certified under the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework. Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR, our interest in knowing how the site is used, in a way that does not identify visitors.

Votes

You can vote on posts without an account. When you vote for the first time, your browser stores a random code under the key openshelf-voter, and your votes under openshelf-votes, in local storage. This lets you change or take back a vote and keeps you from being counted twice. Nothing is stored before you vote (§ 25(2) no. 2 TDDDG).

Our server stores the post, your vote, a scrambled form of the random code and the date. To limit abuse, it also stores a code computed from your IP address and the date, which allows only a few votes per network and post each day. This code is erased after one day. Your IP address itself is not stored. Vote totals are shown publicly on the site.

Clicking the same arrow again takes your vote back and deletes it from the server. Otherwise votes are kept. Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR, our interest in letting readers show which posts they find useful, with protection against manipulation.

Submissions and removal requests

Submissions and removal requests are made through GitHub issues. Anything you write in an issue is public, and GitHub's privacy statement applies. We use what you send only to handle your request. Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR.

Posts and their authors

Openshelf lists research posts that are publicly available on websites. They are found through the websites' own feeds, Hacker News (through the Algolia search API) and Rogue Scholar. Our crawler identifies itself as openshelf-crawler and links to the About page.

For each post we store its title, address, website, date and topic, short summaries in English and Chinese, discussion counts from Hacker News, and which other websites link to it. We do not collect authors' names separately, but a name can appear in a web address, a title or a summary. We also keep a list of addresses already checked, so that no post is checked twice. Entries are kept until their removal is requested.

To decide whether a post is research and to write the summaries, the public text of the post (its description and roughly the first 320 words, and for responses the text around the link) is sent to language models through OpenRouter (OpenRouter, Inc., USA). OpenRouter passes it on to model providers that may be located outside the EU, for example in the USA or China. Only this public text is sent, never anything about visitors.

Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR. The interest is to make research published on personal websites easier to find, and to show how posts answer each other. Only what the authors have published themselves is used, and they can object at any time.

To object or to have a post removed, send a removal request. Once we have processed it, the post is deleted from the data and the site. If you ask, we also stop collecting from your whole website. Earlier versions of the data remain in the history of the public repository. Tell us if you want them removed there as well.

Your rights

You have the right of access (Art. 15 GDPR), rectification (Art. 16), erasure (Art. 17), restriction of processing (Art. 18) and data portability (Art. 20), and the right to object to processing based on legitimate interests (Art. 21). To use them, see the contact above.

You also have the right to lodge a complaint with a data protection supervisory authority (Art. 77 GDPR), in particular in the EU member state where you live or work.
